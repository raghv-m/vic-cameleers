Design brief · D2

The Caravan Route

Vic Cameleers redesign, for approval before any code. Reading this as: a local-service landing site for people in Melbourne's south-east planning a move on their phone, with a warm outback-freight language, leaning toward Tailwind 4 tokens, one stencil display face and a plain industrial body face, and restrained, motivated motion.

Dials: variance 7, motion 6, density 4. Audit verdict before this: REDESIGN, 15/30 (Dieter Rams scorecard in DESIGN-IS-2026-09-26/).

One idea

The site is a single journey. A dotted caravan route starts in the hero, draws itself down the page as you scroll, pauses at each section like a stop on a trip, and ends at the quote button. Three moments carry the site: the headline stamping in like a freight label, the route drawing itself with a small truck riding it, and prices rolling like an odometer. Everything else stays still.

Colour

The brand's own hues, kept. One change: the CTA terracotta is deepened from #c1502e to #b34628, because the current buttons fail WCAG AA (4.41:1). Terracotta is only for things you're meant to click. 70% sand, 20% navy, 10% terracotta.

Type

Two families, self-hosted through next/font, subset to Latin, font-display: swap. Tabular figures on every price so digits don't jump when they roll.

Option A
Zilla Slab display + Barlow body
Removalists in Clyde North
$480 to $660

A friendly, bookish slab. Reads well at every size. Closest to the SEO report's suggestion, but more "newsletter" than "freight".

Option B
Roboto Slab display + Public Sans body
Removalists in Clyde North
$480 to $660

Solid and neutral, very legible. Also very common: it's the slab most sites default to, so it adds the least character.

Option C · recommended
Big Shoulders Stencil display + Barlow body
Removalists in Clyde North
$480 to $660

A condensed stencil cut from industrial Chicago lettering, the closest free face to stencilled crate lettering. Condensed, so the headline fits two lines on a 390px phone at 44px. Barlow was drawn from highway and signage lettering, so the pair speaks the same road-and-freight language, and Barlow has tabular figures for prices. Stencil is used only for headlines and freight labels; anything under 24px is Barlow.

Surfaces

  • Radius 4px everywhere: crates, not bubbles.
  • Hard 2px navy borders on the few panels that need separating (calculator, invoice example). No soft shadows anywhere.
  • Paper grain: one inline SVG noise layer, fixed, pointer-events: none, 4% opacity, under 2 KB.
  • Kraft cardboard band behind the fleet section only.
  • One tape or torn edge per page, maximum.
  • Freight labels on 3 of the 9 homepage stops, not every section.
Photo: 10 tonne truck, side viewPlaceholder, 4:3, 1600 x 1200. Real photo drops in from one config file.

Photos: every slot is a labelled sand-and-kraft block at its final aspect ratio, with its path and alt text in one config file (src/config/photos.ts) and a shot list in docs/photo-shot-list.md. No stock or AI photos of trucks, crew, homes or customers. There's no image-generation tool in this environment, so the grain, route line, south-east map and the 1860 archival panel will be hand-built as SVG in the brand palette, not generated images.

Hero headline

Five options only this business could say. None of them implies the business itself dates from 1860.

  1. Same job as the 1860 cameleers. Better suspension.
    Recommended. Short, a bit funny, sets up the story, and fits two lines on a phone.
  2. The camels retired. The cameleers didn't.
    Strong line, but needs the story to land. Better as the story section's headline.
  3. Two trucks, ten movers, zero camels.
    Every word is a checkable fact. Good for the fleet section.
  4. Cameleers hauled Melbourne's freight in 1860. We brought trucks.
    Clear, but long, and less of a laugh.
  5. Your couch, carried like it's 1860. Driven like it's now.
    Warm, but "carried like it's 1860" can read as old-fashioned.

Subtext (under 20 words): Cranbourne removalists moving homes across Melbourne. $120/hr, 2 hour minimum. See your price below.

Homepage: 9 stops

Down from 12 sections. Each stop makes one point, once.

0. Hero

Truck photo slot full-bleed with a navy overlay on the left. Headline, subtext, Call and Get a free quote on the left. The live calculator panel overlaps the photo on the right; on a phone it sits directly under the headline, inside the first screen. The route starts here.

1. Proof strip

One thin row: ABN, 6 and 10 tonne trucks, Cranbourne base, 2 hour minimum. The Google rating joins it once real. Replaces the trust strip, the "difference" section and the review trust cards.

2. What we move

A freight manifest instead of 8 cards: services listed as rows on the left, one large photo slot on the right that swaps to the hovered or tapped service, with a roller-door reveal. Photo-led without 8 zigzag rows.

3. How it works

One merged version, three stops on a short branch of the route: Get your price, Pick your day, We move you. No 01 to 04 badges.

4. The crew

Hidden until real crew profiles exist. When they do: a strip of faces and names, duotone to colour on hover.

5. The cameleer story

The one dark navy section. An archival-style 1860 panel (hand-drawn SVG route map), three short paragraphs, ending on "Same idea, bigger trucks." The only place the camel appears large.

6. Fleet

Two truck photo slots side by side on a kraft band, each with a "what fits" outline diagram whose items load in on scroll.

7. Where we move

A stylised south-east Melbourne map (SVG) with Cranbourne pinned and the 8 published suburbs as chips, pins dropping in.

8. FAQ and final quote

FAQ on the left, a big "Get your price in 60 seconds" panel on the right. The route ends at its button.

Travel16 to 24 pxNever more
  • Nothing starts invisible. Content is readable at first paint; motion only settles it into place. (Today, two sections render blank until scrolled.)
  • Framer Motion is already installed; GSAP (ScrollTrigger, MotionPath) is added for the route line only, bundled locally, loaded only on pages that draw a route. Budget: under 60 KB gzipped.
  • Every effect has a reduced-motion fallback. No custom cursors, scroll-jacking, particles, typewriter text, 3D tilt, marquees or confetti.

Where the skills and the playbook disagreed

  • Radius and glass. The high-end skill wants pill buttons, glass and 2rem rounded shells. The playbook's 4px radius, hard borders and no glass win.
  • Reveals. Skills want elements to fade up from invisible. Here, content is visible first and only settles.
  • Services layout. The playbook says alternating photo rows; the taste skill caps zigzags at 2 in a row. The manifest list plus one swapping photo keeps it photo-led without 8 zigzags.
  • Eyebrows. The playbook says freight labels "sparingly"; the taste skill says at most 1 in 3 sections. Labels go on 3 of the 9 stops.
  • Placeholder photos. Skills suggest picsum or stock. Your rule wins: labelled slots only.
